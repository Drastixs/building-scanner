# Public Architectural Source Search: Buildings 1-30

Research date: 6 June 2026. Reference building: Arbor, Bankside Yards, 255
Blackfriars Road, London SE1 9AX. Distances are approximate walking
distances retained from the frozen building list.

This catalogue records public, non-sensitive architectural sources only. It does
not reproduce or describe internal plans, entrances, staff areas, security
systems, access controls, or service routes. Links that primarily expose internal
layouts have intentionally been omitted.

Entries 1-30 are documented below. **Blue Fin Building (entry 4) and Bankside 1
(entry 5) are two names for the same physical building and must not be counted
as separate buildings.**

## 1. One Blackfriars

- **Identity:** One Blackfriars, 1 Blackfriars Road, London SE1 9PB.
  Predominantly residential mixed-use development; the main tower within the
  three-building One Blackfriars development. Approximately **0.3 miles** from
  Arbor.
- **Size evidence:** Multiplex reports a **170 m, 50-storey** tower, 274 homes
  and three buildings:
  [Multiplex](https://www.multiplex.global/projects/one-blackfriars/). The
  planning proposal covered **74,925 sq m gross external area**:
  [GLA planning report](https://docslib.org/doc/138424/one-blackfriars-in-the-london-borough-of-southwark-planning-application-no-12-ap-1784).
- **Team/status/planning:** Completed; architect Ian Simpson Architects, now
  SimpsonHaugh; developer/client St George plc. Southwark application
  **12/AP/1784**.
- **Public architectural sources:** The Multiplex page provides project facts,
  team and exterior photographs. [WSP](https://www.wsp.com/en-GB/projects/one-blackfriars)
  provides a structural engineering description and photographs.
  [NLA](https://nla.london/projects/one-blackfriars) provides an architectural
  overview. The GLA report records the proposal, massing, uses and planning
  reference. The [London Tall Buildings
  publication](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/ad_103_london_tall_buildings_publication.pdf)
  records the published height and architect.
- **Evidence gaps:** Southwark's current planning search did not surface
  reliably through general web indexing. The GLA report supplies the reference,
  but the council application landing page was not independently retrieved.
- **Availability:** `verified public`; council landing page `not found`.

## 2. Southbank Tower

- **Identity:** Southbank Tower, 55 Upper Ground, London SE1 9EY. Planning
  records also use King's Reach Tower, Stamford Street, SE1 9LS. Mixed
  residential, office and retail use; a refurbishment and vertical extension
  of the former tower and podium. Approximately **0.4 miles** from Arbor.
- **Size evidence:** Mace reports **42 storeys**, 193 apartments and **370,000
  sq ft** of refurbished office space:
  [Mace](https://www.macegroup.com/projects/south-bank-tower). Published counts
  vary between 41 and 42 depending on counting convention.
- **Team/status/planning:** Completed. Original tower by Richard Seifert;
  redevelopment by Kohn Pedersen Fox for developer CIT. Southwark application
  **11/AP/1071** initially covered six added storeys; later amendments increased
  the extension.
- **Public architectural sources:** The [Southwark Planning Committee
  report](https://moderngov.southwark.gov.uk/documents/s21003/Report.pdf)
  provides the official proposal, reference and planning assessment. The [GLA
  Stage 1 report](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/public%3A/public%3A/PAWS/media_id_187605/kings_reach_tower_report.pdf)
  provides strategic planning and massing evidence. The Mace page gives
  construction facts and exterior photography. The [KPF planning announcement
  via e-architect](https://www.e-architect.com/london/kings-reach-tower)
  describes the design. [Yuanda](https://www.yuanda-europe.com/en/projects/southbank-tower/)
  documents facade quantities and external treatment.
- **Evidence gaps:** A definitive planning reference for the subsequent
  five-storey increase was not established during this pass.
- **Availability:** `verified public`; later amendment reference `not found`.

## 3. Sea Containers House

- **Identity:** Sea Containers House, 18-20 Upper Ground, London SE1 9PD.
  Hotel, offices, restaurants and retail; refurbishment of the original 1978
  building plus a new nine-storey office element. Approximately **0.4 miles**
  from Arbor.
- **Size evidence:** tp bennett reports **347,000 sq ft** for its office project
  and describes the planning scheme as a **28,000 sq m office building and
  358-bedroom hotel**:
  [architecture case study](https://tpbennett.com/work/offices/sea-containers-house/)
  and [planning case study](https://tpbennett.com/work/planning/sea-containers-house-planning/).
- **Team/status/planning:** Completed; original design by Warren Platner,
  refurbishment and planning by tp bennett for Archlane Ltd. The hotel opened
  in 2014 and headquarters offices were occupied in 2016. Southwark application
  **11/AP/1955**.
- **Public architectural sources:** The [official Southwark decision
  record](https://moderngov.southwark.gov.uk/ieDecisionDetails.aspx?Id=2454)
  provides the planning reference, height and approved description. The
  [committee report](https://moderngov.southwark.gov.uk/documents/s22715/Report%20Sea%20Containers%20House.pdf)
  supplies planning evidence. The two tp bennett pages provide project scope,
  townscape context and exterior photographs.
  [HDR](https://www.hdrinc.com/ca/portfolio/sea-containers-house) provides a
  refurbishment overview and photography.
- **Evidence gaps:** The two tp bennett area descriptions cover different
  project scopes and should not be treated as directly interchangeable.
- **Availability:** `verified public`.

## 4. Blue Fin Building

- **Identity:** Blue Fin Building, 110 Southwark Street, London SE1 0SU/SE1
  0TA. Office building with ground-floor commercial uses. It is **Bankside 1**,
  the largest building in the Bankside 123 development, and therefore the same
  physical building as entry 5. Approximately **0.4 miles** from Arbor.
- **Size evidence:** The RIBA architect directory reports **697,000 sq ft GIA**
  and a 13-storey atrium:
  [RIBA](https://find-an-architect.architecture.com/allies-and-morrison-llp/london/bankside-123).
  Current leasing material reports approximately 445,000 sq ft rentable area
  and 12 floors. The figures use different measurement and counting
  conventions.
- **Team/status/planning:** Completed in 2005-2007 and subsequently
  refurbished; architect Allies and Morrison, developer Land Securities
  Properties. The original masterplan reference was not verified. Later minor
  application **21/AP/2026** concerns a commercial unit and is not the original
  consent.
- **Public architectural sources:** The RIBA record provides development
  history, area, facade concept and completion. [Allies and Morrison's model
  study](https://www.alliesandmorrison.com/models/bankside-123) shows public
  facade studies. [NLA](https://nla.london/projects/bankside-123-canvey-street)
  provides development scale, completion and team.
  [Foreman Roberts](https://foremanroberts.com/project/blue-fin-building-bankside-london/)
  provides development-wide gross area and engineering context. [Turner &
  Townsend alinea](https://www.ttalinea.com/project/blue-fin-building/)
  documents the recent refurbishment.
- **Evidence gaps:** No authoritative, indexed landing page for the original
  planning consent was found.
- **Availability:** `verified public`; original planning reference `not found`.
