# Finding Arbor / Bankside Yards Architectural Drawings via Public Planning Records

A study guide for accessing the *publicly published* design drawings and statements
for Arbor (Bankside Yards, Blackfriars Road, Southwark SE1) — the legitimate route
for an architecture student studying entrances, circulation, and front-of-house vs.
back-of-house planning.

## Why this route (not "blueprints")

Fully detailed internal/security plans of an occupied building are deliberately not
published. But UK planning law makes the *design submission* a public record. Those
submissions contain the floor plans, sections, elevations, and — most usefully — the
**Design & Access Statement (DAS)**, which narrates the reasoning behind entrance
placement, circulation flow, and the separation of public vs. servicing/back-of-house
space. That reasoning is what you actually want to study.

## Step-by-step

### 1. Open the Southwark planning register
- Southwark Council planning register search:
  https://www.southwark.gov.uk/planning-environment-and-building-control/planning/view-planning-register-and-comment-planning
- This is the official "Planning Online" register. Free, no login.

### 2. Search by these known references
Bankside Yards is the redevelopment of the former **Ludgate House** (245 Blackfriars
Road) and **Sampson House** (64 Hopton Street). Arbor is the first completed building
(known in the masterplan as Building 3 / a "concept" office). Useful references to
search:

| Reference | What it is |
|-----------|-----------|
| `12/AP/3940` | Earlier Ludgate/Sampson permission (historic baseline) |
| `17/AP/2286` | Minor material amendment — added office floors to "Ludgate C", adjusted "Sampson D" |
| `18/AP/2442` | Main comprehensive Bankside Yards application (Sampson + Ludgate sites) |

Search each reference in the register. If a reference has aged out of the primary
register, use the **address search** instead: "Bankside Yards", "Blackfriars Road
SE1", or "Hopton Street SE1".

### 3. Open the Documents tab
On the application page, go to **Documents / Associated Documents**. Filter or scan for:
- **Proposed Floor Plans** (per level) — room layout + how spaces connect
- **Proposed Sections** — how floors stack, double-height entrance volumes
- **Proposed Elevations** — how the entrance meets the street
- **Design & Access Statement (DAS)** — read this first; it explains the *why*
- **Planning Statement** — context and use mix
- **Transport / Servicing Statement** — back-of-house logistics, loading, staff access

### 4. Read the DAS for your specific questions
Map your interests to DAS sections:
- **Entrances** → "Access", "Public Realm", "Ground Floor Strategy"
- **How rooms connect** → "Circulation", "Floorplate Strategy", "Core Layout"
- **Comfort vs. staff/practicality** → "Servicing", "Back of House", "Amenity"

### 5. Cross-reference the published architecture
For higher-quality images and the architect's narrative:
- **PLP Architecture** (architect) — portfolio page for Arbor / Bankside Yards
- **Native Land** (developer) — journal/press releases with plans and concept diagrams
- **Dezeen, ArchDaily, Building Design (BD), Architects' Journal** — coverage with
  plans, sections, and architect interviews

### 6. Deepen the general skill
The patterns you're studying are taught generically:
- **Neufert, *Architects' Data*** — standard reference for how room types are
  dimensioned and connected
- **Louis Kahn's "served vs. servant spaces"** — the canonical framing for your
  comfort-vs-staff distinction
- Search terms: "back of house planning", "circulation core office building",
  "ground floor active frontage strategy"
- **RIBApix / RIBA Library** — free for students; drawing collections on entrances
  and circulation

## Notes
- The "Documents" set varies per application; later amendments often hold the most
  current drawings. Check the newest reference first.
- Planning drawings are *design intent* at submission, not as-built — perfect for
  studying design reasoning, which is the goal here.
